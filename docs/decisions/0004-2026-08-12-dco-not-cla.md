# ADR 0004 — DCO for contributions, not a CLA

**Date:** 12 August 2026
**Status:** Accepted
**Decider:** Alex Nimmo Smith, Nimmo Smith Technologies Limited

---

## Context

Accepting outside contributions needs some way of confirming a contributor
had the right to submit what they're contributing. The two common approaches
are a Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO) - a per-commit sign-off - or a
Contributor License Agreement (CLA), a separate agreement contributors sign
before contributing.

---

## Decision

Use the **Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO)** rather than a CLA.

---

## Consequences

**Positive:**
- Lightweight for contributors - a `git commit -s` sign-off, rather than a
  separate agreement to review and sign before a first contribution.
- Contributors retain full copyright and control over their own
  contributions, rather than assigning or broadly licensing rights to a
  single entity - a lower-friction, more inviting basis for a community
  project.

**Negative / trade-offs:**
- Because no single entity holds broad rights over the combined codebase,
  changing the project's licence in future - for whatever reason - would
  require tracking down and getting agreement from every contributor, since
  their contributions remain licensed only under the terms in place when
  they submitted them.
